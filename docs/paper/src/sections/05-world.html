World model and maps

The agent knows only what the stock client knows, rebuilt from packets, and it walks only where the client would let it walk, from map files we had to reverse-engineer first. Everything it experiences is handed on to the memory store ().

A mirror of the client, not a better client

The world model (harness/world/) is a packet-driven copy of the client's state. It is not a game engine and not a cheat: its ground truth is the stock client's own handlers, decompiled, and the rule is that the harness never acts on anything the client has already forgotten. The field layouts came from the decompiled NativeAOT client plus upstream ClassicUO and Razor sources, and every field carries a confidence label: upstream, decomp or unknown. Layouts branch on a protocol version dword set during the handshake. Live Outlands reports 12, and many packets are longer than upstream (0xF3 world item 26→38 bytes, 0xC0 effect 36→52, 0x20 mobile update 19→28, and so on) docs/WORLDMODEL.md.

Declarative tables (layouts.py) feed one bounds-checked big-endian reader; procedural parsers cover list and flag packets. WorldRuntime dispatches 46 server-to-client and 17 client-to-server packet ids into a StateStore: self (position, vitals, gold, weight, facet, skills), mobiles, items as a container tree, open gumps (UO's server-defined dialog windows), the target cursor, buffs and names: "Last-write-wins entity snapshots plus the lazy name map." Client traffic counts as input too: a walk request stays pending until the server's 0x22 confirm, and the self position moves only on confirms, which matched the proxy's own position at all 23,652 settled points of a replay 1520d7e.

Each change also comes out as an event: the world model emits item_seen, delete, speech_heard, death, …, and the proxy's MoveAuthority adds its own step and blocked events. The proxy keeps the newest 5,000 in a ring for state-port readers and hands every one to the memory store, which keeps them all and derives walk evidence from the steps and denies (). The overseer's "journal" is just a filter over that stream (ctl journal).

The 18-tile view range was a surprise: Outlands sends c8 12, against ClassicUO's default of 24.

Forgetting was the subtle part. On 2026-10-01 the overseer cast Lightning at mongbats that the model still held and the client had long dropped, and the server answered "That is too far away." Since then the model prunes exactly as the decompiled World.ProcessDeletes does: on the roughly once-a-second 0xFF sub 5 tick and on every self move, drop whatever lies beyond the view range set by S2C 0xC8. All 395 range prunes in one session landed within −0.68 to +0.06 s of the client's own close-status packet for the same serial docs/WORLDMODEL.md "Pruning".

A cautionary footnote: on 2026-09-28 we spent an evening documenting an Outlands "world-state stream" of 106-byte records that turned out, the next afternoon, to be decode garbage from a skipped XOR (). With the corrected decode, all 18 captures (53,277 packets) parse with zero failures.

Upstairs, and the map in 34 minutes

Until 2026-09-29 the harness walked by a 2-D "walk memory": tiles and edges it had seen the server confirm. The first live lumber attempt ended that era. At 21:23 the character TestWorth left its rental room and came out upstairs in the Shelter Island inn, at (1938, 2584, z 20). The walk memory knew only the ground floor at those x, y, so "A* kept routing through upstairs walls": 21 blocked moves, then ABORTED: too many blocked moves. Worse, the 40 upstairs blocks it had learned would have poisoned ground-floor routes, so that change was reverted docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md §13.

Outlands ships none of the standard map*.mul, statics*.mul or tiledata.mul files; the client reads proprietary .uoo containers through its own UOO.* classes. An agent read those loaders in Ghidra and committed a working mmap reader at 21:49, 26 minutes after the abort, with 42 checks against capture evidence (tree targets, inn floors at z 0 and 20, stairs, a sunken area at −20). The 3-D pathfinder followed at 21:57 f92f680 216f26c. lists the differences from the classic formats.

Facet 0, the main world, holds 10,773,929 statics. Two findings shaped the rest of the code. Facet 3 is a blank placeholder of impassable land, yet the rental rooms live there, so the old walk memory survives as the planner for rooms. And doors are not statics at all but dynamic items sent by the server docs/MAP.md.

Walking like the client

harness/pathfind.py ports ClassicUO's Pathfinder.cs (CreateItemList, CalculateMinMaxZ, CalculateNewZ, CanWalk), with z widened to int as in the Outlands build. The land surface is "stretched" between its four corner heights (top = own tile, right, left, bottom), and the walkable average takes the flatter diagonal:

z_\text{avg} = \begin{cases} \lfloor (z_\text{top}+z_\text{bottom})/2 \rfloor & |z_\text{top}-z_\text{bottom}| \le |z_\text{left}-z_\text{right}| \\ \lfloor (z_\text{left}+z_\text{right})/2 \rfloor & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}

A capture confirmed it: the server put us at z 10 on a tile whose raw land z is 11 (corners 11/10/10/10). new_z then chooses, among the land, surfaces and bridges on the target tile, the one whose height is closest to the current z and leaves 16 units of clearance (one body) under the next blocker. The proof set: all 1,249 server-confirmed moves in the walk memory are walkable, all 20 non-door denies upstairs are walls, and a route from upstairs to the innkeeper exists. The same Walkers object also gives the proxy a z for every confirmed step, because 0x22 confirms carry none; before that, the proxy's z was wrong at 12 of 57 live anchors d602ea8.

Planning is plain heap A* over (x, y, z) with 8 neighbours. A diagonal costs the same as a straight step, as on the server. Costs are shaped rather than forbidden:

MOBILE_COST_X  = 4.0    # tile occupied by a mobile (they move)
DANGER_COST_X  = 30.0   # inside a creature's danger zone
SHOVE_RETRY_S  = 15.0   # a denied shove makes the tile a wall this long
MOBILE_WAIT_S  = 90.0   # a route cut only by mobiles is waited out
max_expand     = 30000  # A* node budget (3-D)

Per-plan humanizer noise scales these, and nav.straighten irons out zig-zags (agent paths had 37% heading changes against 21% for a human). Server denies become blocked (x, y, dir) moves for the session. Each step is re-checked with CanWalk against items that arrived after planning, because the stock client never sends a step it would refuse. Doors are opened ahead with the stock request; teleporters are learned by stepping (a landing that isn't the next tile) into the teleporters table. The map knows terrain but not spawns; the blind 250-tile goto through a harpy nest belongs to .

Houses are multis

"Player houses block walking, and the map files don't show them." A house arrives as an 0xF3 item whose graphic indexes multi.mul, not art, and until 2026-10-02 the harness ignored its pieces. At Corpse Creek, house 0x154 drew 12 server denies on one trip, each step walkable on the bare map. Two bugs were stacked: the walk rules didn't expand multis, and the model pruned the house the instant it arrived at 22 tiles. The fix expands each 16-byte multi.mul record (graphic, dx, dy, dz, flags) into dynamic objects and keeps a house within view range plus its reach, as ClassicUO's HouseManager.IsHouseInRange does. Now the house is present at all 12 deny points, and routes enter houses by the front door docs/NOTES.md "Houses".

We rejected learning houses from denies: "It costs a deny burst per house, which no client produces, and a house can be placed or removed at any time." The last rule came from a death on 2026-10-05: an escape route ran through another player's door ("you can't walk into one uninvited", as the owner put it). Now every door on or beside a house we are not standing in is locked to the planner.

Rune libraries: reaching the whole map

A rune marks a spot; casting Recall on it teleports you there. A rune tome or runebook holds many, and each recall spends mana or one of the book's charges.

Walking from banks reached little: 354 of 359 public treasure-map runes lie more than 110 tiles from every bank. The community "Witcher" rune set (runes 1–353 plus letter suffixes; a public table of 359 dig tiles) stands as 14 public tomes in the Cambria Rune Library. On 2026-10-04 Outland Dan's guild library at the DTF house turned out to be much bigger: 36 locked-down tomes, 839 runes.

The trick that made libraries cheap was reading without recalling. In the rune-tome gump, row i's button 100+i recalls (and spends a charge), but 200+i opens the rune's detail page, which shows each rune's name and its tile "(x, y)". Button 5 pages on. ctl act read_tomes dtf read all 36 tomes in 463 s, every tile, "with nothing cast and no charge spent." Runebooks show only sextant strings, which convert as

x = 1323 + \text{lon}\cdot\tfrac{5120}{360}, \qquad y = 1624 + \text{lat}\cdot\tfrac{4096}{360}

(east and south positive, wrapped), within one tile. Reading also showed that the guild's Witcher runes had been marked by someone else: 58 of 359 land away from the public table's tile (301 exact, 31 off by 1–5 tiles, 23 by 6–17, 3 by 18–40; rune 165 by 100). Hence the decision "a tome row's own tile is the only truth": one library table with rows per tome, and ambiguous names (20 repeat across DTF tomes) refused rather than guessed docs/PLAN.md "Rune libraries".

Home is data, not code. harness/data/homes.json maps each character to a landing (Outland Dan's book's default rune, the "DTF Loot Chest" at (4134, 1429, z 6)), its library, and a facet-3 rental room reached through the house steward. home.py counts anything within 60 tiles of the landing as home.
