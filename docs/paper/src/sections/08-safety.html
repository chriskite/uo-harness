Staying alive

The harness never fights a player, so survival is a matter of noticing early and leaving fast. This chapter shows how it tells a player killer from a goat, how the escape grew from “stop” to a recall that recasts until it lands, and how each of thirteen deaths became a rule within hours. It also covers the thief guard and the one runner that is allowed to fight. The CAPTCHA solver and the staff alarm, which keep a harvest job going rather than a character alive, are part of the lumberjack loop (, ).

The stance: never fight back

Notoriety is the colour of a name: blue innocent, grey attackable or criminal, orange enemy, red murderer, yellow invulnerable (code 7). A PK (player killer) is a red who kills other players for their loot. See .

The threat research of 2026-09-29 opens with one rule: “The harness never attacks a player.” Hitting a player brings Heat of Battle, which blocks recall (the one fast way out), makes you criminal and draws staff attention docs/research/THREATS.md §0. Everything below is detection and evasion, never retaliation.

The data for that evasion came partly from accidents and partly on purpose:

Let's bank what we've got and go somewhere potentially more dangerous and see if we can get PK'd or killed by mobs for science (data to improve our agent harness)

— the owner, viz chat to the overseer, 2026-10-02 17:27

Two hours later a red named Bastet killed Hackworth in the Terran wilds, and the owner replied by phone: “that's exactly the data we were hoping for”.

Reading a mobile: the threat classifier

Outlands gives no clean “this is a PK” signal. Monsters show grey, mongbats turn criminal, human-bodied NPCs exist, and a pickpocket is blue until he steals. threats.assess() is a pure function of one state snapshot, so it can be unit-tested and replayed over captures harness/threats.py.

identify() decides what a mobile is, in order: a ghost is ignored; notoriety 7 is an NPC; mobile flag 0x20 or a “(Young)” label makes a player; a non-human body named “a/an …” is a monster; a “Name the title” label is an NPC; a human body with a creature name and no flag is a monster at notoriety 3 (the orc-hunter fix, 0add311). Any other human body counts as a player. The 0x20 flag is an empirical find: by 10-04 it was on 1,474 human mobiles at notoriety 1/3/4/6 and never on 2,607 notoriety-7 sightings or about 2,400 non-humans docs/PLAN.md "Staff alarm".

Movement turns Chebyshev distance into an arrival estimate, \text{ETA} = \max(0, d - r_s)\cdot s, with s = 0.2 s/tile for a player running, 0.1 mounted and 0.4 for a monster, and strike range r_s = 12 tiles for a player (an assumption: RunUO's spell range; the Outlands value is unsourced), 1 for a monster. The flee radius is the distance at which a recall still lands first:

R_\text{flee} = r_s + \left\lfloor \frac{t_\text{recall} + t_\text{margin}}{s} \right\rfloor

The design example, a 4 s recall and 1 s margin against a red on foot, gives 12 + 25 = 37 tiles, past the 18-tile view, “so any visible red means flee”. The deployed runner uses a 2 s recall and 1 s margin: 27 tiles against a red on foot, 42 against a mounted one.

Creatures are judged by aggression: swinging or casting at us, war-mode flag 0x40 unless the body or name is on a passive list, known aggressive bodies, else not aggressive. That default changed on 09-30 after lumber trips stopped for a walrus and a goat c7dc39c.

Damage was harder than it sounds. “Outlands has never sent one with us as the defender”: all 3,645 swing packets (0x2F) in the store at the snapshot (2,172 when this was found on 10-03) are our own, so the live damage signal is the hits trend. Spells are caught by their visual effects on us or by lines such as “Magic reflect removed.”; all 93 such signals in the captures came with an attack. Since the server never names an attacker, hit_attackers blames adjacent creatures first, then ranged ones in reach, then anything within 12 tiles.

Bastet, three times

The same red killed Hackworth three times in 24 hours, and each encounter broke a different assumption ().

Terran wilds, 10-02 19:43. Bastet rode into view at 18 tiles. The runner aborted at 0.29 s and then stood still: its only response to danger was an Unsafe stop. “Bastet is attacking you!” came at 2.80 s; the first hit at 4.58 s carried a hamstring (no running for about 3 s); five hits and 102 damage later Hackworth was dead at 10.96 s. The “5…1” countdown over Bastet's head was most likely an explosion-potion fuse inferred: a blast followed 1.2 s after “1”, after the death: “Weapon, not explosion.” Tracking never fired, and the runner's ETA guess of 0.6 s was eight times too pessimistic docs/NOTES.md "Bastet".

That last number flipped the design. The one live runebook recall on record took 2.09 s from button to arrival, and Bastet needed 4.58 s from sight to first hit. “So a recall started within ~2.4 s of sight lands before the first hit. … A recall is the only action with a chance, so it wins.” Recall-on-sight shipped at 22:16 that night f7aab09.

Nusero, 10-03 18:04. Bastet appeared at 11 tiles carrying the Stationary Penalty, which suggests he had just recalled in or had stood hidden inferred. The new escape reacted fast, starting a recall at 1.05 s, but his Weaken landed at 1.88 s and broke it. The instant retry was refused as “not recovered”, his Harm broke the third try, and the three-try escape gave up at 3.64 s. Then nothing hit Hackworth for 4.5 s (Bastet's potion blew on his own tile, two swings missed), and no escape was running to use it. The verdict: “A mage PK who recalls onto us beats a 2 s recall escape”.

The commit and NOTES say Bastet “struck 5.5 s later” after the tracking hit. The DB event clock gives 6.51 s to “attacking you” and 7.59 s to the first hit. We suspect the note was rounded from a different anchor.

Terran wilds again, 10-03 19:08. This time Tracking reported “Bastet (55 spaces to target)”, but the react range was 40 tiles, so it was only logged. He came into view 4.57 s later, during the chop's 2.1 s aim pause. The runner answered the chop cursor first and opened the tome 2.5 s after sight; the first hit at 3.02 s broke the cast, and Hackworth died at 8.39 s. The real bug: “The runner was blind inside every pause and result wait”, because Human.wait was a plain sleep c4db365. The owner first asked by phone for a 60-tile react range; offered 40, 80 or any by the agent, he chose 80 4017175.

The escape, v1 to v3

The escape (harness/escape.py) presses the default rune's charge in the runebook or rune tome and falls back to casting Recall. It was rebuilt twice, each time from measurements.

v2 (10-03 18:27) came from a study of the casts in the store docs/research/SPELL_INTERRUPTS.md. Recall takes a median 2.03 s from power words to arrival (1.95–2.09 s, 27 casts). Creature melee breaks a cast 44% of the time (26/59), damage-over-time ticks never (0/13), Bastet 3/3. After a disturbed cast, all 30 live retries fit RunUO's pre-AOS recovery rule, where e is how far into the cast it broke and T the cast time:

t_\text{rec} = \max\!\left(0.2,\; 1 - \sqrt{e / T}\right)\ \text{s}

So v2 has no try limit, only a 20 s budget, and presses each retry the moment recovery ends. Replayed on Nusero, the old code gives up 2.73 s after the double-click (live: 2.72 s); the new one lands at 5.0 s d6e6c63. “A PK has to land a fresh interrupt within every 2 s cast, and giving up hands him the time.”

v3 (10-05) learned the failure that killed Outland Dan at Wintertop, “This book needs time to recharge.”: a recharging book is now answered by casting Recall on the book itself, which worked live at 10:59 87b53fe. On 10-06 a world save held a recall's landing to 5.18 s, past the 5 s arrival wait; the retry failed and Dan died at the landing (D13), so the arrival wait now runs at least 10 s past our power words ade127e.

In the store, 31 of 34 logged lumber escape recalls landed: 30 by book charge, 2.09–2.20 s from press to arrival (median 2.13 s), and one by Recall cast on the book. Two of the three logged failures were Bastet, under the old three-try code; the third was D12, a monster read as a grey player, whose one cast was disturbed 1.4 s in. D9's escape failed before a cast (a recharging book) and is not in that count. Of the 14 escape recalls a player triggered, 12 landed; a pk_escape juncture is written only after a successful escape, so the twelve such junctures (five of them reds that Tracking reported at 35–64 tiles, four on 10-05 and one on 10-06) are clean by construction memory DB job_events recall, junctures. The newest player escape shows the limit of “landed”. On 10-06 at 11:38 a mounted mage with bonded pets came into view under a blue name, spoke the words of Explosion 3.1 s later and turned grey 3.9 s after that, 5 tiles off, with “… is attacking you!”. The runner pressed the book 0.38 s after that notice and landed home, but the delayed blast went off on Dan just after the landing (100 → 69 hits) docs/NOTES.md, ee59524. If a recall fails off guarded ground, a guard flight runs A* to guard points learned from “now protected” notices at the tile where they appeared 4cb8b9c.

Lesson

Early on, false positives cost trips: a walrus, a goat, a battle trainer. From 10-05, false negatives were killing Dan: an unknown failure message, a house door in an escape route, an 8.93 s humanizing pause, a monster read as a player, a landing delayed by a world save. Each fix made the runner more cautious; lumber flee events rose from 2–13 a day (09-30 → 10-04) to 44 on 10-05 (10 more on 10-06 by noon), the price of the owner's final policy: “run and recall until home, never stopping in the field”.

Thieves: step away, and a trapped pouch

At 100 Stealing a thief takes about 200 logs per grab, once every 5 s, from 1 tile away. The one we met, Caputo Wood, was blue until the instant the reagents left the pack (T1). The owner's summary, “thieves look friendly to us until they steal, and so a friendly player being within 2 tiles of us AT ALL while we're lumbering is almost certainly a thief”, became the 2-tile rule 2516411. Attacking him is out, so the guard has three layers ad5a6c0:

  1. Keep-away. The runner pauses as if reading, then walks to ≥ 4 tiles: “One step beats the 5 s steal cooldown, where a 2 s recall would still leave them in reach.” If he closes again: recall, and a 20-minute cooldown on that spot.
  2. Trapped pouch. Logs go into a trapped pouch after each chop. Its pop was decoded from our own set-off (hue 38 → 0, an explosion effect, a sound, −1 hit) a929050; one of our own pouches going off when we did not open it is an alarm, while an explosion sound alone is not, since it can be someone else's pouch or a spell 5e70fad. “A pouch is a delay plus an alarm, not a lock.”
  3. Ledger. Losses become theft events that feed the optimizer's theft hazard ().

Live, keep-away fired twice on 10-05, both for mounted blues at 2 tiles. Neither came closer and nothing was stolen; we suspect passers-by. Both pouch alarms so far were false: F2, and F3 on 10-06, when a PK's Explosion going off at the home landing sounded like a pouch. T1 is still the only theft, and it was never booked as a theft event.

The one runner that fights: hunt loop and crawl

After the overseer let Hackworth fall to 2/96, fighting moved into a deterministic runner, loop_hunt.py f5008fd. Each tick it takes the first of Leave → Speech hold → Heal → Stationary Penalty → Loot → Fight → Idle that applies. It leaves for a hostile player within 12 tiles, for two or more attackers below 80% hits, or below 60% plus a margin for the walk out, and heals with a potion when it can, else by spell.

The Stationary Penalty is an Outlands debuff: it appeared 301.0–314.8 s after the last step in all 40 measured cases, and 5 steps clear it.

One fight spot yields only 0.4–0.5 kills a minute and invites the Stationary Penalty, so the crawl patrols the New Player Dungeon instead ee62f02. Its floor graph has 10,829 tiles, up to 182 route steps deep, cut into five depth zones with 147 waypoints. Zone 1 starts at the store's measured 23.4 hits lost per minute; each deeper zone's prior is the one above times a depth risk of 1.5; and a zone opens only when its predicted damage is at most 30 hits/min. Since 23.4 \times 1.5 = 35.1 > 30, zone 2 stays shut until zone 1 proves cheaper than about 20 hits/min.

Across 10-02 and 10-03 the hunt runner made 36 visits (265.7 minutes) for 173 kills and 2,785 gold. It left 32 times (18 for low hits, 9 for multiple attackers, 5 done) and died 0 times. The crawl has run live in zone 1 only.

What still kills us

Nusero set the open problem: a stealthing mage who appears at 11 tiles and casts within 1.9 s beats a 2.03 s recall. docs/PLAN.md "PK survival" lists three answers. Detecting stealthers: Hackworth's Tracking sees hidden players only within about 8 tiles. Magic Reflection: at Nusero it would have bounced the Weaken, and recall 1 would have landed 0.49 s before his Harm; Hackworth lumbered with it up on the evening of 10-03, when it absorbed a gazer larva's spell (N3). Mounted flight with scripted healing helps only with a guard zone or line-of-sight break in reach. None has met a real PK yet. The 10-06 mage showed a milder form of the same problem: his power words under a blue name triggered nothing until his attack notice, and a spell already cast followed the recall home. The system survives the threats it has met, and it learned each one by dying to it first.
