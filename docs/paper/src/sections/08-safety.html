Staying alive

The harness never fights a player, so survival is a matter of noticing early and leaving fast. This chapter shows how it tells a player killer from a goat, how the escape grew from “stop” to a recall that recasts until it lands, and how each of eleven deaths became a rule within hours. It also covers the thief guard, the staff alarm, the CAPTCHA solver and the one runner that is allowed to fight.

The stance: never fight back

Notoriety is the colour of a name: blue innocent, grey attackable or criminal, orange enemy, red murderer, yellow invulnerable (code 7). A PK (player killer) is a red who kills other players for their loot. See .

The threat research of 2026-09-29 opens with one rule: “The harness never attacks a player.” Hitting a player brings Heat of Battle, which blocks recall (the one fast way out), makes you criminal and draws staff attention docs/research/THREATS.md §0. Everything below is detection and evasion, never retaliation.

The data for that evasion came partly from accidents and partly on purpose:

Let's bank what we've got and go somewhere potentially more dangerous and see if we can get PK'd or killed by mobs for science (data to improve our agent harness)

— the owner, viz chat to the overseer, 2026-10-02 17:27

Two hours later a red named Bastet killed Hackworth in the Terran wilds, and the owner replied by phone: “that's exactly the data we were hoping for”.

Reading a mobile: the threat classifier

Outlands gives no clean “this is a PK” signal. Monsters show grey, mongbats turn criminal, human-bodied NPCs exist, and a pickpocket is blue until he steals. threats.assess() is a pure function of one state snapshot, so it can be unit-tested and replayed over captures harness/threats.py.

identify() decides what a mobile is, in order: a ghost is ignored; notoriety 7 is an NPC; mobile flag 0x20 or a “(Young)” label makes a player; a non-human body named “a/an …” is a monster; a “Name the title” label is an NPC; a human body with a creature name and no flag is a monster at notoriety 3 (the orc-hunter fix, 0add311). Any other human body counts as a player. The 0x20 flag is an empirical find: by 10-04 it was on 1,474 human mobiles at notoriety 1/3/4/6 and never on 2,607 notoriety-7 sightings or about 2,400 non-humans docs/PLAN.md "Staff alarm".

Movement turns Chebyshev distance into an arrival estimate, \text{ETA} = \max(0, d - r_s)\cdot s, with s = 0.2 s/tile for a player running, 0.1 mounted and 0.4 for a monster, and strike range r_s = 12 tiles for a player (an assumption: RunUO's spell range; the Outlands value is unsourced), 1 for a monster. The flee radius is the distance at which a recall still lands first:

R_\text{flee} = r_s + \left\lfloor \frac{t_\text{recall} + t_\text{margin}}{s} \right\rfloor

The design example, a 4 s recall and 1 s margin against a red on foot, gives 12 + 25 = 37 tiles, past the 18-tile view, “so any visible red means flee”. The deployed runner uses a 2 s recall and 1 s margin: 27 tiles against a red on foot, 42 against a mounted one.

Creatures are judged by aggression: swinging or casting at us, war-mode flag 0x40 unless the body or name is on a passive list, known aggressive bodies, else not aggressive. That default changed on 09-30 after lumber trips stopped for a walrus and a goat c7dc39c.

Damage was harder than it sounds. “Outlands has never sent one with us as the defender”: all 3,638 swing packets (0x2F) in the store at the snapshot (2,172 when this was found on 10-03) are our own, so the live damage signal is the hits trend. Spells are caught by their visual effects on us or by lines such as “Magic reflect removed.”; all 93 such signals in the captures came with an attack. Since the server never names an attacker, hit_attackers blames adjacent creatures first, then ranged ones in reach, then anything within 12 tiles.

Bastet, three times

The same red killed Hackworth three times in 24 hours, and each encounter broke a different assumption ().

Terran wilds, 10-02 19:43. Bastet rode into view at 18 tiles. The runner aborted at 0.29 s and then stood still: its only response to danger was an Unsafe stop. “Bastet is attacking you!” came at 2.80 s; the first hit at 4.58 s carried a hamstring (no running for about 3 s); five hits and 102 damage later Hackworth was dead at 10.96 s. The “5…1” countdown over Bastet's head was most likely an explosion-potion fuse inferred: a blast followed 1.2 s after “1”, after the death: “Weapon, not explosion.” Tracking never fired, and the runner's ETA guess of 0.6 s was eight times too pessimistic docs/NOTES.md "Bastet".

That last number flipped the design. The one live runebook recall on record took 2.09 s from button to arrival, and Bastet needed 4.58 s from sight to first hit. “So a recall started within ~2.4 s of sight lands before the first hit. … A recall is the only action with a chance, so it wins.” Recall-on-sight shipped at 22:16 that night f7aab09.

Nusero, 10-03 18:04. Bastet appeared at 11 tiles carrying the Stationary Penalty, which suggests he had just recalled in or had stood hidden inferred. The new escape reacted fast, starting a recall at 1.05 s, but his Weaken landed at 1.88 s and broke it. The instant retry was refused as “not recovered”, his Harm broke the third try, and the three-try escape gave up at 3.64 s. Then nothing hit Hackworth for 4.5 s (Bastet's potion blew on his own tile, two swings missed), and no escape was running to use it. The verdict: “A mage PK who recalls onto us beats a 2 s recall escape”.

The commit and NOTES say Bastet “struck 5.5 s later” after the tracking hit. The DB event clock gives 6.51 s to “attacking you” and 7.59 s to the first hit. We suspect the note was rounded from a different anchor.

Terran wilds again, 10-03 19:08. This time Tracking reported “Bastet (55 spaces to target)”, but the react range was 40 tiles, so it was only logged. He came into view 4.57 s later, during the chop's 2.1 s aim pause. The runner answered the chop cursor first and opened the tome 2.5 s after sight; the first hit at 3.02 s broke the cast, and Hackworth died at 8.39 s. The real bug: “The runner was blind inside every pause and result wait”, because Human.wait was a plain sleep c4db365. The owner first asked by phone for a 60-tile react range; offered 40, 80 or any by the agent, he chose 80 4017175.

The escape, v1 to v3

The escape (harness/escape.py) presses the default rune's charge in the runebook or rune tome and falls back to casting Recall. It was rebuilt twice, each time from measurements.

v2 (10-03 18:27) came from a study of the casts in the store docs/research/SPELL_INTERRUPTS.md. Recall takes a median 2.03 s from power words to arrival (1.95–2.09 s, 27 casts). Creature melee breaks a cast 44% of the time (26/59), damage-over-time ticks never (0/13), Bastet 3/3. After a disturbed cast, all 30 live retries fit RunUO's pre-AOS recovery rule, where e is how far into the cast it broke and T the cast time:

t_\text{rec} = \max\!\left(0.2,\; 1 - \sqrt{e / T}\right)\ \text{s}

So v2 has no try limit, only a 20 s budget, and presses each retry the moment recovery ends. Replayed on Nusero, the old code gives up 2.73 s after the double-click (live: 2.72 s); the new one lands at 5.0 s d6e6c63. “A PK has to land a fresh interrupt within every 2 s cast, and giving up hands him the time.”

v3 (10-05) learned the failure that killed Outland Dan at Wintertop, “This book needs time to recharge.”: a recharging book is now answered by casting Recall on the book itself, which worked live at 10:59 87b53fe.

In the store, 18 of 20 logged lumber escape recalls landed (17 of them 2.09–2.16 s from press to arrival); both logged failures were Bastet, under the old three-try code. D9's escape failed before a cast (a recharging book) and is not in that count. Of the 10 player-triggered escape recalls, 8 landed; a pk_escape juncture is written only after a successful escape, so the eight such junctures (the last four on 10-05, when Tracking finally reported reds at 35–64 tiles) are clean by construction. If a recall fails off guarded ground, a guard flight runs A* to guard points learned from “now protected” notices at the tile where they appeared 4cb8b9c.

Lesson

Early on, false positives cost trips: a walrus, a goat, a battle trainer. By 10-05, false negatives were killing Dan: an unknown failure message, a house door in an escape route, an 8.93 s humanizing pause. Each fix made the runner more cautious; lumber flee events rose from 2–13 a day (09-30 → 10-04) to 31 on 10-05, the price of the owner's final policy: “run and recall until home, never stopping in the field”.

Thieves: step away, and a trapped pouch

At 100 Stealing a thief takes about 200 logs per grab, once every 5 s, from 1 tile away. The one we met, Caputo Wood, was blue until the instant the reagents left the pack (T1). The owner's summary, “thieves look friendly to us until they steal, and so a friendly player being within 2 tiles of us AT ALL while we're lumbering is almost certainly a thief”, became the 2-tile rule 2516411. Attacking him is out, so the guard has three layers ad5a6c0:

  1. Keep-away. The runner pauses as if reading, then walks to ≥ 4 tiles: “One step beats the 5 s steal cooldown, where a 2 s recall would still leave them in reach.” If he closes again: recall, and a 20-minute cooldown on that spot.
  2. Trapped pouch. Logs go into a trapped pouch after each chop. Its pop was decoded from our own set-off (hue 38 → 0, an explosion effect, a sound, −1 hit) a929050; a pop we did not cause is an alarm. “A pouch is a delay plus an alarm, not a lock.”
  3. Ledger. Losses become theft events that feed the optimizer's theft hazard ().

Live, keep-away fired twice on 10-05, both for mounted blues at 2 tiles. Neither came closer and nothing was stolen; we suspect passers-by. The only pouch alarm so far was F2, a false one.

Staff: an alarm that has never rung for real

Staff are the one encounter the agent must not handle alone: “a player unresponsive to a GM for ~2 minutes while gathering is jailed, then banned on repeat.” Since 09-30 anyone speaking nearby holds a harvest job until a human or the overseer acks 38c427d, and staff hints in that speech raise gm_suspected with a repeating alarm 021bcec. On 10-04 the owner suggested a GM is probably an invulnerable player, so sight alone should alarm. No staff_sighting exists in the store, and no staff alarm has fired since 10-02. A GM shown blue or grey, or hidden, would still get past the sight rule, so the speech hold remains the backstop.

The CAPTCHA solver

Harvesting raises a CAPTCHA every 5–10 minutes: a dialog with a three-digit number and a box to type it into. Three wrong answers block harvesting for 6 hours, and the shard can also read response time and long-run accuracy as detection signals ANTICHEAT.md §3, §8.8. So a wrong answer costs far more than a slow one, and the design follows from that. A human answers by default (the runner pauses and beeps), the solver sits behind a toggle in the viz header, and the solver is allowed to refuse. This subsection describes the solver for readers who want to build one: what the input looks like, the algorithm, how it was labelled and evaluated, and where it can still fail.

Finding the real CAPTCHA among decoys. Every lumberjacking attempt also opens a decoy dialog that carries the CAPTCHA's words ("Captcha", "Type the Value") under a fresh random gump id, with no reply button and flags that keep it off the screen. The human never sees one and the stock client cannot answer one, so any reply to a decoy can only come from automation inferred: a honeypot for bots that match on text ANTICHEAT.md §8.13. The store holds 5,087 of them at the snapshot, none with a reply button, and the harness's audits found no reply to any. The real CAPTCHA is gump id 1, with text entry 2 (at most 3 characters), a Guide button 1 and one more reply button, the submit, whose id changes with every CAPTCHA (594, 843, 528, 606, …). The first detector keyed on 594 and missed the second CAPTCHA it ever saw. Detection now requires gump id 1, text entry 2 and exactly one reply button other than Guide, and never looks at text harness/captcha.py:78-85.

The input is a point set, not an image. The digits are not pictures. Each digit is drawn as 9–18 tilepic elements, small item graphics placed at explicit layout coordinates, so the gump packet itself lists every dot position. The graphic changes from CAPTCHA to CAPTCHA (35 different ones across the 53 real CAPTCHAs) and carries no information. The digit is the arrangement of the dots, which are displaced from a fixed shape and sometimes include strays (, left). Reading a digit is therefore classification of a small 2-D point cloud, and there is nothing to OCR. The plan had a screen-crop classifier or a small CNN in reserve in case the glyphs ever came as pixels ANTICHEAT.md §8.8. With 7 labelled CAPTCHAs (21 digits) when the solver was built, a nearest-neighbour matcher over point sets fit the data better: it needs no training, its decisions can be inspected one reference at a time, it can say "not sure", and each new labelled CAPTCHA adds three references with no retraining.

The algorithm harness/captcha.py:

  1. Segment. Collect the tilepic coordinates (at least 18), sort them by x and cut at the two largest gaps in x, which gives three clusters. Each cluster must pass sanity gates (6–25 dots, a height of 40–120 px, a width of at most 120 px; the real ones have 10–17 dots, 63–82 px and 35–80 px). Anything else is not treated as a CAPTCHA.
  2. Normalize. Centre each cluster on its centroid and divide by its height, so position and size drop out but width and slant stay: \tilde p = (p - \bar p)/h.
  3. Compare. Measure the distance from the cluster A to each reference B with a symmetric, trimmed Chamfer distance, minimized over small translations:
    D(A,B) = \min_{\delta\in\Delta}\ \tfrac12\Big[\,T_{0.85}\big\{\min_{b\in B}\lVert a-b-\delta\rVert\big\}_{a\in A} + T_{0.85}\big\{\min_{a\in A}\lVert a-b-\delta\rVert\big\}_{b\in B}\Big]
    T_{0.85} is the mean of the smallest 85% of the nearest-neighbour distances, and \Delta is a 9 × 9 grid of shifts within ±0.09. Chamfer distance needs no one-to-one matching of dots, which matters because the dots are displaced and their count varies. Trimming the worst 15% in each direction ignores strays, and the shift search absorbs the centroid error that strays and missing dots cause harness/captcha.py:97-115.
  4. Classify, or refuse. The cluster takes the digit of its nearest reference among the 79 in the font, at distance D_1. Let D_2 be the distance to the nearest reference of any other digit. The margin
    m = \frac{D_2 - D_1}{D_2}
    must be at least 0.09 for all three digits, or the solver returns nothing and the human gets the CAPTCHA harness/captcha.py:118-142. Comparing against the nearest other digit, rather than the second-nearest reference, matters: two references of the same digit agreeing is not ambiguity.

Each digit costs 79 references × 81 shifts in pure Python, about a third of a second on the desktop, so about a second per CAPTCHA, hidden inside the deliberate human-paced delay described below.

Labels from the server. Labelled data came for free, because the server says whether an answer was right. harness/captcha_mine.py replays session captures, pairs every real CAPTCHA gump with the 0xB1 answer to it and the server's "Captcha successful." within 10 s, and keeps only accepted answers. Their three clusters become new references. Before adding anything, it reports whether the font as it was would have solved each new CAPTCHA, and with what margin, so every addition first serves as an out-of-sample test harness/captcha_mine.py:1-21. The dataset grew from 7 CAPTCHAs on 09-30 to 26 on 10-03 (12 sessions); the font has 79 references, 78 real plus one synthetic 0, with 4 to 15 per digit.

Evaluation. lists every test, from weakest to strongest evidence. Grouping matters in the held-out test. Digits from one session tend to look alike, so the test holds out whole sessions, not single digits. A leave-one-digit-out test would have been optimistic.

What the gate does and does not do. The margin protects against ambiguity: a cluster that lies about as close to a 3 as to an 8 is refused. It does not protect against novelty. The 10-01 failure shows the difference: a 9 unlike any reference was simply nearer to an 8 than to anything else, by a comfortable margin. This is the open-set problem every nearest-neighbour classifier has, and the defences against it here are outside the classifier. First, the font grew from real data: the second real 9, which the owner called "real funky" (86 px wide, with strays on both sides), was read right out of sample at margins above 0.6, and with it 194 reads correctly. Second, a human answers by default. Third, a rejected answer is noticed: the server opens a fresh CAPTCHA, the solver reads that one, and after its third rejection it hands over (--captcha-max-strikes 2 counts the rejections it carries on after) harness/loop_lumber.py:1768-1808, 3464. Three rejections are also the shard's block threshold, so this last line of defence leaves no slack; it has never been tested, because no answer was ever rejected. The digit 0 is the open case. Its only reference is a synthetic oval ring, because none of the 159 digits seen so far was a 0. If the shard drew digits uniformly from 0–9, the chance of that would be 0.9159 ≈ 5 × 10−8, so it almost certainly draws from 1–9 inferred, and the synthetic 0 has never been tested.

A synthetic score that fell as the solver improved. The 0.09 gate was set on the jitter test: with the first 21 references it accepted 99.2% correctly and refused 12% harness/captcha.py:17-24. Each later batch of real references lowered that number, to 98.1% with 37, 97.2% with 73 and 96.98% with 79, while every real out-of-sample test improved. Real digits vary more than the synthetic jitter does, so a jittered reference lands closer to other digits' real shapes as the font fills up inferred. On 10-03 the test's bar was lowered from 97% to 96%, and the real-data tests became the ones that count ANTICHEAT.md §8.8 "Dataset 26".

Answering like a person. A reading is not sent at once. The runner waits a lognormal delay with a median of 11.5 s (σ 0.38, stretched 12% per hour of play), chosen from the owner's measured solves of 7.7–17.5 s. It then checks that the client has not answered meanwhile, so one gump never gets two answers, and sends the stock 0xB1 with every text entry filled as the client would. Finally it waits up to 12 s for "Captcha successful." or a fresh CAPTCHA, which counts as a strike harness/humanize.py:52; harness/loop_lumber.py:1768-1808. shows the whole path.

The outcome. 53 real CAPTCHAs, 53 answers, 53 “Captcha successful.”, 0 strikes. The solver answered 43 (3 on 10-01, 11 on 10-02, 29 from 10-03 on) with a median of 13.25 s from gump to answer; the owner answered 10 with a median of 10.21 s. By design the solver is a little slower than the person it imitates.

Lesson

Four things carried this solver, and none of them was model capacity. The input was read from the protocol instead of the screen, which turned OCR into point-set matching. The model was one that can refuse: a nearest-neighbour vote with a margin, whose every decision can be traced to a reference. The server labelled the data, and each new label was first used as an out-of-sample test. And the evaluation that counted was real and grouped by session, while the synthetic benchmark drifted the wrong way. The margin handles ambiguity, not novelty; the policy around the solver (a human by default, a strike budget) is what handles a digit it has never seen.

The one runner that fights: hunt loop and crawl

After the overseer let Hackworth fall to 2/96, fighting moved into a deterministic runner, loop_hunt.py f5008fd. Each tick it takes the first of Leave → Speech hold → Heal → Stationary Penalty → Loot → Fight → Idle that applies. It leaves for a hostile player within 12 tiles, for two or more attackers below 80% hits, or below 60% plus a margin for the walk out, and heals with a potion when it can, else by spell.

The Stationary Penalty is an Outlands debuff: it appeared 301.0–314.8 s after the last step in all 40 measured cases, and 5 steps clear it.

One fight spot yields only 0.4–0.5 kills a minute and invites the Stationary Penalty, so the crawl patrols the New Player Dungeon instead ee62f02. Its floor graph has 10,829 tiles, up to 182 route steps deep, cut into five depth zones with 147 waypoints. Zone 1 starts at the store's measured 23.4 hits lost per minute; each deeper zone's prior is the one above times a depth risk of 1.5; and a zone opens only when its predicted damage is at most 30 hits/min. Since 23.4 \times 1.5 = 35.1 > 30, zone 2 stays shut until zone 1 proves cheaper than about 20 hits/min.

Across 10-02 and 10-03 the hunt runner made 36 visits (265.7 minutes) for 173 kills and 2,785 gold. It left 32 times (18 for low hits, 9 for multiple attackers, 5 done) and died 0 times. The crawl has run live in zone 1 only.

What still kills us

Nusero set the open problem: a stealthing mage who appears at 11 tiles and casts within 1.9 s beats a 2.03 s recall. docs/PLAN.md "PK survival" lists three answers. Detecting stealthers: Hackworth's Tracking sees hidden players only within about 8 tiles. Magic Reflection: at Nusero it would have bounced the Weaken, and recall 1 would have landed 0.49 s before his Harm; Hackworth lumbered with it up on the evening of 10-03, when it absorbed a gazer larva's spell (N3). Mounted flight with scripted healing helps only with a guard zone or line-of-sight break in reach. None has met a real PK yet. The system survives the threats it has met, and it learned each one by dying to it first.
