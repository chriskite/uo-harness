Self-optimization: where to chop and how much to carry

The runner works one spot; a pure-function planner decides which spot, how many logs per trip and which hatchet, using a small Bayesian model of place, character and danger, a renewal-reward trip size and Thompson sampling. In its latest snapshot it is deliberately exploring: no spot has more than a 3.0% chance of being the best, and nearly every trip is sized by the grove, not by the hazards.

Objective and architecture

The objective: boards stored at home per agent-active hour, net of expected losses docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md:177. One log makes one board. Pacing is a floor, not a knob: the optimizer picks places and trip sizes but never tightens the delays, breaks or daily cap that the runners' pacing rules fix.

harness/lumber_opt.py (1,613 lines at ee59524; first commit 5a33805, 2026-10-02 23:26) is a pure function plan(...) over plain lists, fed by plan_from_store. It returns one runner command, e.g. ctl run lumber --spot witcher_244 --trips 1 --logs-per-trip 3970 --regrow-min 65 --timeout 9924, so "the overseer, a future daemon or a human run the same decision" docs/PLAN.md:488-489. The Seer () asks ctl lumber plan before each run. It does not always run the pick: during the Seer6 shift of 2026-10-05 small explore groves gave 262–392 boards a trip, so from run 14 on the overseer ran the greedy spot instead, with the pick's trip arguments and --spot docs/NOTES.md:816-824 (ec339a1), and the 2026-10-06 morning shift did the same (chat: "Greedy pick: seek ye witcher_40", "seek ye witcher_23, the greedy grove"). Since 89130d8 (2026-10-06 10:03) the plan also carries the greedy spot's own run, and --exploit makes the pick itself greedy.

Spots, recency and the character

A spot is only a place: an area, its trees, a PvP flag and a hazard_prior. Since 2026-10-04 every trip starts and ends at home, so getting there is not part of it. Evidence is weighted by recency, so shifts in competition, killers or patches show up within weeks:

w_i = 0.5^{\,\text{age}_i / 14\ \text{days}}

Place and character are modelled separately. Outland Dan's Lumberjacking rose from 50.1 to 95.6 in about a day and a half (), so a plain per-spot average would favour whichever spots were visited last. The success chance per attempt follows the wiki, summed over the ten wood colours a tree can be:

A hatchet's material and quality add a tool bonus that multiplies the chance: Exceptional +0.04, Bronze +0.08, Avarite +0.18 docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md:81-82.
\begin{gathered} p(S,b) = \min\Big(1,\ \sum_{j}\tfrac{\text{base}_j}{100}\,\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\Big(\tfrac{S-\text{off}_{j'}}{\text{div}_{j'}}\,(1+b)\Big)\Big),\\ j' = j \text{ if } S\ge \text{min}_j,\ \text{else ordinary} \end{gathered}

S is skill, b the tool bonus, basej the chance a tree is wood j (81.75% ordinary to 0.25% avarwood); a colour above our skill counts as ordinary, an inference the code flags harness/lumber_opt.py:337-356. It checks out: Hackworth at 69.1 hit 44/63 at Terran (0.70) against 0.69 predicted docs/NOTES.md:362-364. Each trip's chopping time t_c is rescaled to today's chance; walking is not:

H_{\text{adj}} = \max\!\big(F - t_c + t_c\,p_{\text{then}}/p_{\text{now}},\ 0.1 F\big),\qquad H_{\text{adj}}\ge 0.25\ \text{h for a place failure}

with F the field time. The floor makes a trip the place spoiled (no reachable tree, a town region) cost a quarter hour at zero logs, so a dud spot loses its optimistic prior harness/lumber_opt.py:499-510. The Harvest Aspect (armour that sometimes doubles a yield) is left to recency weighting.

Field rate and overhead

The field rate \lambda_s is logs per field hour, walking between trees included. Logs come in lumps (7.92 per success over 4,125 successes), so trips scatter far beyond Poisson; a quasi-Poisson dispersion \phi (Pearson \chi^2/dof over trips of 3+ minutes, floor 8) was 14 on 2026-10-02, 26.8 on 2026-10-05 20:26 and 17.0 at the snapshot harness/lumber_opt.py:725-742. Discounting the evidence by \phi:

\lambda_s \sim \text{Gamma}\Big(\alpha_0+\tfrac{1}{\phi}\textstyle\sum_i w_i L_i,\ \ \beta_0+\tfrac{1}{\phi}\sum_i w_i H_{\text{adj},i}\Big),\qquad \alpha_0=\mu^2/s_0^2,\ \ \beta_0=\mu/s_0^2

The prior is empirical Bayes: \mu is the mean rate of spots with half a weighted field hour, s_0^2=\max(\widehat{\operatorname{Var}},(0.35\mu)^2), and 1,200 logs per field hour before any data harness/lumber_opt.py:825-838. An unvisited spot is "a spot like the others", explored but not trusted. Tree density was rejected as a prior term ("it didn't predict logs per field hour across the 7 spots with trips"); it returns as capacity.

Overhead T is everything but chopping. Its prior, in seconds:

T_0 = \underbrace{10}_{\text{room exit}} + 0.45\,(\text{library walk} + \text{walk in}) + \underbrace{2\cdot 4}_{\text{recalls}} + \underbrace{60}_{\text{lockout}} + \underbrace{15}_{\text{into room}} + \underbrace{40}_{\text{convert, store}}

The posterior is a weighted Normal counting the prior as one observation; the per-trip spread is floored at 30 s and the posterior sd is that spread over \sqrt{n}, so it can fall below 30 s harness/lumber_opt.py:704-722, 857-862. Overheads are short: 102 s at Horseshoe Bay, 139 s at witcher_23.

Three ways a trip ends early

The rewrite of 2026-10-03 (86075f3) split danger into three competing hazards per field hour, each a per-spot Gamma posterior over weighted exposure, shrunk to a pooled rate:

  • Death h_D: nothing stored, every unblessed item lost, plus an inferred R=20 minutes of recovery.
  • Sent home h_S: a recall escape, guard flight or creature stop (); the load comes home.
  • Theft h_T: a thief takes a share f\sim\text{Beta}(1,1) of the load and the trip goes on.

Deaths are rare, so their prior is built from commoner events: hostile sightings per field hour \sigma_s, times a pooled \pi=P(\text{death}\mid\text{sighting}), plus a pooled creature-death rate h_C:

\begin{gathered} h_D \sim \text{Gamma}\big(10\,m_0 + \textstyle\sum w\cdot\text{died},\ 10 + E_s\big),\qquad m_0 = \sigma_s\,\pi\,[\text{pvp}] + h_C,\\ \pi\sim\text{Beta}(1+pk_w,\ 3+\text{seen}_w-pk_w) \end{gathered}

E_s is weighted field hours. A death counts against a spot if it falls in a trip there or within 30 minutes after ("the Terran PK killed us 11 s after the runner stopped") harness/lumber_opt.py:517-551, 892-915, 1119-1126. Prior strengths are in . The same PK deaths count twice: in the pooled \pi prior and in the spot's own death count harness/lumber_opt.py:869, 894-895, 1119. That empirical-Bayes double use makes h_D at the death spots (Terran, witcher_23, witcher_282, witcher_196) somewhat overconfident while deaths are this few.

How much to carry: from a square root to renewal-reward

On 2026-09-29, before the first live trip, we set the return trigger with a textbook inventory argument, the economic-order-quantity trade-off : at r logs per minute, overhead T and death hazard h_D, the cost per stored log is

\text{cost}(Q)=\frac{rT}{Q}+\frac{h_D Q}{2r}+h_D\,T_{\text{back}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad Q^*=r\sqrt{2T/h_D}

The rule's hazard is death only, "PK encounters × P(death)" docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md:190: being sent home loses nothing, so it has no place in a cost per stored log, and with h_D+h_S the rule would give sizes of 288–646. Its flaws, named at the rewrite: it credited a dying trip with Q/2 banked, its expected deaths h_D Q/\lambda exceed 1 on long trips, and Q sat on a 60-minute stint cap docs/PLAN.md:501-504. The replacement treats a trip as one renewal-reward cycle (). Chopping Q logs takes t_f=Q/\lambda; with h=h_D+h_S and theft thinning the load at k=h_Tf:

\begin{aligned} C(t) &= \frac{\lambda\,(1-e^{-kt})}{k}, \qquad B(Q) = e^{-ht_f}\,C(t_f) + h_S\!\int_0^{t_f}\! e^{-ht}C(t)\,dt,\\ P_D &= h_D\!\int_0^{t_f}\! e^{-ht}\,dt, \qquad \E[\text{time}] = T + \int_0^{t_f}\! e^{-ht}\,dt + R\,P_D,\\ \text{Rate}(Q) &= \frac{B(Q) - \text{supplies} - G\,P_D}{\E[\text{time}]} \end{aligned}

G is the gear at risk (unblessed items at replacement price); it and the supplies are priced in logs at the board price. Every integral has a closed form, so P_D\le 1 for any Q; that is why "a clamp on deaths" and Monte Carlo per Q were both rejected docs/PLAN.md:509-510. best_q scans 41 log-spaced sizes from 200 to 10,000 logs plus the weight cap, then refines by golden section to 10 logs harness/lumber_opt.py:797-822.

Finding

The square-root rule was not far off. At the snapshot's means its Q is 7–24% below the renewal-reward optimum (Horseshoe Bay 2,281 vs 2,950; witcher_23 2,793 vs 3,510; Terran 1,260 vs 1,350; witcher_291 1,227 vs 1,610), which costs at most 0.12% of the rate in our recomputation. The rewrite fixed the accounting, not the size: at Q^*, P(\text{death}) per trip is 3–9% but P(\text{sent home}) is 69–91%. Threats, not deaths, end most long trips.

That shapes the curves: being sent home costs only the next trip's overhead, so "being sent home alone never shrinks Q (nothing is lost); deaths, thefts and the gear at risk do" docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md:336-337. On the 2026-10-03 store the rewrite changed sizes by up to ×1.6 on eligible spots (×2.2 on the Young-only Shelter spot), mostly by lifting them off the stint cap, and net rates fell as sent-home stops were charged honestly docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md:395-407.

Grove capacity and regrowth

The owner's point on 2026-10-03: a sparse grove dries up sooner, so its trip pays full overhead for fewer logs. The runner ends a trip dry once its listed trees are chopped, so a trip holds at most

\begin{gathered} \text{grove\_logs} = \text{avail}\times \psi\times L_{\text{tree}},\\ \psi \sim \text{Beta}\big(10 \psi_0+y,\ 10(1-\psi_0)+n-y\big),\qquad \psi_0=\frac{\sum y+1}{\sum n+2} \end{gathered}

avail counts tree tiles not depleted, unreachable or marked out of wood within the regrowth window, y of n tried tiles ever yielded, and L_\text{tree} is 19.3 logs per tree cycle, pooled over 471 cycles (17.7–20.2 per spot) harness/lumber_opt.py:622-689. The yielding share \psi varies, 0.47 at witcher_282 to 0.86 at corpse_creek at the snapshot (0.46 to 0.85 on 2026-10-03), and the model matched dry trips (Horseshoe Bay ~677 predicted, dry at 605 and 613). When the cap binds, the run is one trip, and the runner gets the uncapped Q^* "so it chops until the trees really run out instead of stopping at the estimate" (aa1de12).

Regrowth comes from pairs of a depleted answer followed by a later try on the same tree. A pool-adjacent-violators isotonic fit gives P(\text{regrown}\mid\text{gap}); the window is the first block reaching 0.6, rounded up to 5 minutes harness/lumber_opt.py:578-619. It replaced a 20-minute guess that "sent the runner to depleted trees".

Since Smart Harvest (2026-10-04) the server picks the tree, so new data adds no regrowth pairs or tried tiles harness/lumber_opt.py:585-587, 631-634.

The estimate is biased upward by design: "Retry gaps shorter than the window in use are no longer observed, so the estimate can only move up from here unless the window is shortened on purpose" docs/NOTES.md:359-361.

Choosing a spot: Thompson sampling

Thompson sampling picks by drawing from the posteriors. For each eligible spot the planner draws (\lambda, T, \sigma_s, \pi, h_D, h_S, h_T, \psi) once, takes \max_Q \text{Rate} over every other grid point, and the best draw wins. Repeating this 2,000 times gives each spot's P(best); the pick is labelled explore when it is not the best by posterior mean harness/lumber_opt.py:1215-1228, 1259-1261. The theft share f and the creature rate h_C are not drawn: both enter the mean plan and every draw as pooled posterior means harness/lumber_opt.py:908-915. Spots are ineligible for 30 minutes after a death or player threat, 20 after a thief, until regrowth after a dry trip, and 7 days after two place failures in a row.

Decision

Thompson sampling "explores in proportion to how likely a spot is to be the best, needs no tuning constant, and varies the routine". Rejected: ε-greedy ("explores bad spots as often as promising ones, needs an ε schedule"), UCB ("deterministic, so the same spot order every day; a fixed pattern is a bot signature") and an LLM reading the stats ("not reproducible, no uncertainty accounting") docs/PLAN.md:482-489.

Variation is required, not an inefficiency to remove.

Hatchets, wear and the price of a board

Each hatchet we own or could buy at a known price rescales the rate through the chopping share \rho of field time, minus wear at one use per success:

\lambda'=\Big(\frac{\rho}{\lambda}\,\frac{p_{\text{now}}}{p(b)}+\frac{1-\rho}{\lambda}\Big)^{-1},\qquad \text{value}=v-\frac{v}{\text{logs/success}}\cdot\frac{\text{price}}{\text{uses}}\cdot\frac{1}{\text{gp/log}}

where v=\max_Q\text{Rate}(\lambda'), with a bought hatchet's price added to G harness/lumber_opt.py:1023-1039. One use per success was measured twice (a hatchet went 500 → 163 over 337 chops on 2026-10-05). Wear is small: an iron hatchet (25 gp, 500 uses) at 1,700 net logs per trip-hour costs about 11 gp, 0.4 logs, per hour. The bonus is not: the exceptional, mastercrafted bronze hatchet Dan was handed on 2026-10-05 (+0.16; docs/NOTES.md:578-584) lifts p at 80.1 skill from 0.776 to 0.900, about 11% more logs per field hour at a 0.7 chop share. Every carried hatchet is lost on death, so a bought one is "worth it where nobody can kill us, not where they can". Unpriced hatchets get a break-even price instead.

Gold becomes logs at board:ordinary: the wiki's 9.5 gp until 2026-10-05, when the lowest commodity ask put it at 26.8 gp (134,000 gp per 5,000-board deed), and coloured woods were mostly no dearer (dullwood 22, copperwood 20, bronzewood 19; only shadowwood, at 30 gp, beat ordinary). When those commits landed on 2026-10-05, revaluing the 63 trip rows of the time at the asks instead of the wiki's 9.5 gp took their 16,036 logs from 147,773 to 428,442 gp (ba7ade5, b2238c7); that basis prices logs without a wood breakdown as ordinary, while values only the logs with one. For the planner, gear and supplies got cheaper in logs: a 200 gp recall charge is 7.5 logs, not 21.

Caveat

Much of the danger model is still prior: the one observed theft (10-03, Caputo Wood) was never booked as a theft event, and none has been booked since (0 theft events at the snapshot), so h_T and f are pure prior, and R and the room terms of T_0 are guesses. One aborted trip is a false hazard sample, a Harvest-Aspect sparkle read as a spell docs/NOTES.md:602-607. The snapshot is not a live plan either: without a proxy the gear at risk is the last row's hatchet (now the exceptional bronze one, unpriced, so G=0) and its tool bonus is left out of p (0.913 instead of 0.974 at 95.0 skill), and only cached landing routes count.
