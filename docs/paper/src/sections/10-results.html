Results

Eight days, 69 proxy sessions and 72 logged-in hours later, the harness was chopping about 1,200 logs per 22-minute trip with one character, and it had answered every CAPTCHA it saw. This chapter collects the outcome numbers that cut across the other chapters: time, traffic, wood, skill, spot choice, hazards and money. The final section says how far to trust them.

Where the hours went

Session length badly overstates how much the agent played. The proxy captured 72.0 hours of logged-in time: 33.2 h on the Test Shard and 38.8 h on the other shard. Counting only the wall-clock minutes that contain at least one client-to-server (C2S) packet tagged src=agent gives 26.1 agent-active hours. The same count over human-driven opcodes (walk, double-click, target, gump reply) gives 9.3 human-input hours, and the runners' own episodes add up to just 13.1 h.

The shape of follows the project. 09-28 and most of 09-29 were human-only capture sessions, the owner playing while the proxy recorded. Agent traffic starts on 09-29 at session 20260929_142237. The busiest agent day was 10-03 (8.37 agent-active hours), when Hackworth chopped the overworld and Shackleworth hunted. Outland Dan's single 9.65-hour session on 10-05 carried 17,141 agent packets by itself.

"Agent-active" measures the presence of injected traffic, not autonomy. Some of it was the Seer steering through ctl ().

On the wire, the agent sent 75,113 of the 438,450 C2S packets: 17.8 % of the traffic that carries a source tag. The share depends heavily on the opcode. The agent sent 89 % of walk requests (0x02), 98.9 % of target replies (0x6C) and 84.5 % of gump replies (0xB1), but almost none of the background chatter (0.3 % of 0xFF envelopes, no name queries), which kept coming from the stock client. charts the per-day composition. One loose end: recounting the 2026-09-30 detection-surface audit's 11 sessions gives 67,991 C2S packets against the doc's 63,441. The agent count matches exactly (10,503), so the 4,550-packet gap lies in how the client's own packets were counted, by a basis we could not recover.

Which shard?

TestWorth's 30 sessions all went to 74.91.115.123, the Test Shard. There, rooms are wiped daily and skills can be set in a player editor. Every other character (Hackworth, Shackleworth, Outland Dan, Logan Wolf) connected to 35.71.142.123 or 52.223.17.219. The project's own documents disagree about what those addresses are. The lumber doc calls Dan's shard "the live shard" docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md §12.5. ANTICHEAT says which shard those IPs serve is "unconfirmed". NOTES labels Hackworth's data "Test Shard", although none of his sessions ever went to the Test Shard IP. The circumstantial evidence points to production [inference]: multilingual guild recruiters advertising in the field, a real guild house with a rented room, and the user "switching to Test Shard" on 10-02 to get a runebook, which only makes sense if they were somewhere else to begin with. This chapter says "the other shard" and leaves the label at that.

Wood: logs chopped, boards stored

The runners chopped 18,757 logs in 2,400 successful chops, and the 63 trip rows recorded 10,380 boards stored (412 on the Test Shard). The two numbers measure different things, and the gap between them is about what got recorded, not about lost wood. "Chopped" comes from harvest_attempts, which records every chop; "stored" comes from trip rows. Before 2026-10-02 an aborted trip wrote no row at all, so on 10-01 Hackworth chopped 1,044 logs against a single trip row docs/NOTES.md l.369–373. The rows also miss one big store: on 10-05 the overseer finished trip 92 by hand after the convert loop gave up, and the chest then showed 2,747 boards. Once the boards moved to the guild's Resource Stockpile, it read 8,693 across five woods.

Nearly half the wood came on the last day. is flat until Hackworth reached the overworld on 10-01, climbs steadily through 10-02 and 10-03, pauses for the home cutover on 10-04, and then turns almost vertical: 9,162 logs on 10-05 across 30 runner launches. shows the same arc trip by trip.

The baseline is Run 3 on 2026-09-29: 50 boards in 8 min 25 s on Shelter Island, "about 355 boards per agent-active hour" docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md l.1428. Dan's best trips on 10-05 ran at 3,000–3,300 logs per trip-hour; trip 98 brought in 1,198 logs in 21.8 minutes. The other half of the picture is the row of hollow markers along zero. Since 10-02, 30 of 45 trips ended early, 19 of them on a monster or player threat, and 17 of those 30 brought home nothing. That is why Dan's day aggregate on 10-05 is 2,589 logs per trip-hour, well below his best trips.

Where the ninefold came from

Logs per hour of chopping factor cleanly into three terms: how often a chop resolves, how often it succeeds, and how many logs a success yields.

R_{\text{chop}} = \frac{3600}{c}\;\times\;p\;\times\;\bar{y}

Here c is the median chop cycle in seconds, p = success / (success + fail) and \bar{y} is logs per success. On 09-29 those were 8.98 s, 0.31 and 7.93, which implies about 990 logs per chop-hour. On 10-05 they were 5.21 s, 0.72 and 8.07, which implies about 4,020. The measured trip-hour rate is lower on both days, because trips also spend time walking between stands, travelling, converting and storing.

The waterfall in divides the credit three ways.

  • Cycle time, ×1.72. The median cycle sat at 9–10 seconds for six days, then fell to 5.2 s when script-paced chopping went live on 2026-10-04 22:34. Its floor is the server's own 4.17 s chop plus about 0.75 s. shows the gap distributions before and after, and the user's reasoning that every harvester on the shard already chops at script pace.
  • Success, ×2.32. This is the largest factor and the most confounded. The 09-29 number comes from Shelter Island, where harvest chance is roughly halved, at skill 60. The 10-05 number comes from the overworld at skill 52–81. Against the wiki formula, Dan's 14 trips with at least 20 attempts produced 1,070 successes where 1,010.9 were expected (+5.8 %). We suspect the Harvest Aspect armour's skill bonus, which the formula leaves out [inference].
  • Yield, ×1.02. A normal chop gives 5–10 logs. 63 chops, all on 10-04/05, gave 12–20: the Aspect's "double yield" procs.
  • Not in the equation. Smart Harvest (target yourself; the server picks the tree) and home-based trips with about 5–10 % overhead act on the gap between chop-hours and trip-hours.

Skill

Only one of the three curves in shows earned progress. Outland Dan went from 50.1 to 81.2 between 10-04 22:05 and 10-05 20:16: 312 updates over about 1,175 successful chops, or roughly 3.8 successes per tenth of a point. TestWorth's jump to 100 on 09-30 was typed into the Test Shard's player editor, so his 9/9 successful chops afterwards say nothing about training. Hackworth is the puzzle. He climbed from 60.1 to 69.1 on 10-01 and then gained nothing for two more days, through about 7,800 more logs. No document explains it. A total-skill cap or a skill lock are the obvious suspects [inference].

Exploring spots

Choosing where to chop was left to the Thompson-sampling planner, which shipped on 2026-10-02 at 23:26 (). Its last snapshot shows how early in learning it still was. Of 132 candidate spots, 26 had a trip. The 106 unvisited spots held 87.4 % of the probability of being best, the visited ones 11.8 %, because unvisited spots inherit an empirical-Bayes prior (2,316 logs/h) that sits above most measured posteriors (). shows the behaviour that followed.

Before the planner, the first 18 trips went to four seeded venues (Shelter Island 10, Horseshoe Bay 5, Corpse Creek 2, Terran 1). Hackworth's 10-03 trips still mostly revisited known spots; the bend toward the diagonal came with Outland Dan, who went to a new spot on 20 of his 30 trips. That is the design working as intended ("Variation is required, not an inefficiency to remove", docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md l.411). It also means most spot estimates rest on one trip. 18 of the 27 visited spots have exactly one, and per-trip logs are overdispersed by φ = 26.8 against a Poisson count.

Hazards and escapes

The harness lost a character to death ten times, plus once on purpose. None of its goods were stolen. collects the safety outcomes. The incident swimlane, the Bastet timing and the CAPTCHA latency distributions are in .

Two lessons sit behind the table. The recall escape works against creatures and against PKs seen early, but not against a mage who recalls onto you: Bastet's spell landed 1.9 s after he appeared, faster than a 2.0 s cast. That pushed the defence into spot choice and exposure time. And early returns, more than deaths, are what cost wood. At 2.54 sent-home events per field-hour, the expected time before a trip is cut short is about 24 minutes of chopping, and every early return pays the travel overhead again.

Money

Nothing was sold, so every wood figure in gold is a valuation. The 12,784 logs that carry a wood breakdown were 96.2 % ordinary; at the board asking prices seen on 10-05 (ordinary 26.8 gp) they come to about 341k gp. These are asks, not sales docs/research/ECONOMY.md l.315. Real gold moved in small amounts: 3,985 gp spent at vendors in 31 purchases (mostly a Mark scroll, a Recall scroll and a horse), and 2,785 gp earned by the secondary hunt loop over 36 New Player Dungeon visits and 173 kills. Shackleworth's melee earned 16.92 gp/min, Hackworth's magery 6.06.

How far to trust these numbers

  • Small samples. 63 trips; 18 of 27 spots have one trip; all three PK deaths came from one player; the theft model is pure prior.
  • Accounting. Logs chopped ≠ boards stored. Hours nest: logged-in 72.0 ≫ agent-active 26.1 ≫ runner episodes 13.1.
  • Confounded progress. Per-day rates mix characters, skill, venue, pacing, Smart Harvest and the Harvest Aspect; the planner rescales only for skill. The ninefold rise describes where we ended up. It is not an A/B test.
  • A live database. The memory DB was read (read-only) around 20:20–20:30 on 10-05 while a runner was still writing; by 20:38 it held 49 more successful chops than the numbers here.
  • Unresolved: the shard label and the audit's 4,550-packet discrepancy.
