Results

Ten days, 69 proxy sessions and 88 logged-in hours later, the harness was chopping about 1,300 logs per 22-minute trip with one character, and every CAPTCHA it saw was answered: 57 by its solver, 10 by the owner. This chapter collects the outcome numbers that cut across the other chapters: time, traffic, wood, skill, spot choice, hazards and money. All of them are taken at one snapshot, 2026-10-06 12:00 CDT. The final section says how far to trust them.

Where the hours went

Session length badly overstates how much the agent played. The proxy captured 87.6 hours of logged-in time. Counting only the wall-clock minutes that contain at least one client-to-server (C2S) packet tagged src=agent gives 30.8 agent-active hours. The same count over human-driven opcodes (walk, double-click, target, gump reply) gives 10.1 human-input hours, and the runners' own episodes add up to just 17.3 h (12.9 h of lumber trips, 4.4 h of hunt visits).

The shape of follows the project. 09-28 and most of 09-29 were human-only capture sessions, the owner playing while the proxy recorded. Agent traffic starts on 09-29 at session 20260929_142237. The busiest agent day is still 10-03 (8.37 agent-active hours; also the most logged-in hours, 22.6, most of them the idle Test Shard session), when Hackworth chopped the overworld and Shackleworth hunted; 10-05 comes close with 7.72. Outland Dan's 9.65-hour daytime session on 10-05 carried 17,141 agent packets; the evening capture that followed it (16.3 h up to the snapshot, 12.0 h of them on 10-06) carried 21,186.

"Agent-active" measures the presence of injected traffic, not autonomy. Some of it was the Seer steering through ctl ().

On the wire, the agent sent 94,295 of the 517,644 C2S packets: 18.8% of the traffic that carries a source tag. The share depends heavily on the opcode. Measured against all C2S packets of each opcode (untagged ones included), the agent sent 90% of walk requests (0x02; 93% of the tagged ones), 99.2% of target replies (0x6C) and 86.7% of gump replies (0xB1; 89% of the tagged ones), but almost none of the background chatter (0.2% of 0xFF envelopes, no name queries), which kept coming from the stock client. charts the per-day composition. One loose end: recounting the 2026-09-30 detection-surface audit's 11 sessions gives 67,991 C2S packets against the doc's 63,441. The agent count matches exactly (10,503), so the 4,550-packet gap lies in how the client's own packets were counted, by a basis we could not recover.

Wood: logs chopped, boards stored

The runners chopped 32,676 logs in 4,125 successful chops; the 86 trip rows hold 28,448 logs and recorded 23,003 boards stored (412 on the Test Shard). The two numbers measure different things. "Chopped" comes from harvest_attempts, which records every chop; "stored" comes from trip rows. The gap is mostly about what got recorded, but not only. Until 2026-10-02 23:26 (5a33805) an aborted trip wrote no row at all, so on 10-01 Hackworth chopped 1,044 logs against one trip row, and on 10-02 3,374 against seven docs/NOTES.md l.369–373; memory DB. Where the fourfold chop rate came from

The headline 10.4× (Dan's best completed trip, 3,704 logs per trip-hour, against Run 3's 355) compares one trip with a three-trip run. It splits into four factors: the chop rate rose ×4.66 (below), trip-time efficiency (the share of the chop-hour rate that survives into trip-hours) improved ×1.65, the best trip beat Dan's 10-05 day mean by ×1.28, and 09-29's day aggregate (376) sat ×1.06 above Run 3. Like for like, day aggregate against day aggregate, the rate rose 376 → 2,890 logs per trip-hour on 10-05 (×7.7) and to 3,341 in the morning of 10-06 (×8.9).

Logs per hour of chopping factor cleanly into three terms: how often a chop resolves, how often it succeeds, and how many logs a success yields.

R_{\text{chop}} = \frac{3600}{c}\;\times\;p\;\times\;\bar{y}

Here c is the median chop cycle in seconds, p = success / (success + fail) and \bar{y} is logs per success. On 09-29 those were 8.98 s, 0.31 and 7.93, which implies about 990 logs per chop-hour. On 10-05 they were 5.21 s, 0.82 and 8.11, which implies about 4,610; on the morning of 10-06, 5.33 s, 0.98 and 7.94, about 5,270. The measured trip-hour rate is lower on every day, because trips also spend time walking between stands, travelling, converting and storing.

The waterfall in divides the credit three ways.

  • Cycle time, ×1.72. The median cycle sat at 9–10 seconds for six days, then fell to 5.2 s when script-paced chopping went live on 2026-10-04 22:34. Its floor is the server's own 4.17 s chop plus about 0.75 s. shows the gap distributions before and after, and the user's reasoning that every harvester on the shard already chops at script pace.
  • Success, ×2.64. This is the largest factor and the most confounded. The 09-29 number comes from Shelter Island, where harvest chance is roughly halved, at skill 60. The 10-05 number comes from the overworld at skill 52–90, and success kept rising with skill (0.98 on 10-06 at 90–96). Against the wiki formula, Dan's 14 trips with at least 20 attempts up to 10-05 20:30 produced 1,070 successes where 1,010.9 were expected (+5.8%). We suspect the Harvest Aspect armour's skill bonus, which the formula leaves out [inference].
  • Yield, ×1.02. A normal chop gives 5–10 logs. Chops of 12–20, all from 10-04 on, are the Aspect's "double yield" procs.
  • Not in the equation. Smart Harvest (target yourself; the server picks the tree) and home-based trips act on the gap between chop-hours and trip-hours. Home and travel overhead was only about 5% of trip time in Dan's 10-05 daytime trips; most of the roughly 37% gap on 10-05 is time inside the harvest phase that isn't chopping (walking to stands and trees, lockouts, CAPTCHAs, pauses).

Skill

Only one of the three curves in shows earned progress. Outland Dan went from 50.1 to 95.6 between 10-04 22:05 and 10-06 11:55: 456 updates over about 2,890 successful chops. The climb slows as the skill rises: about 3.8 successes per tenth of a point up to 81.2 (10-05 20:16), about 10.5 from 81.2 to 90.5, and about 14 from 90.5 to 95.6. TestWorth's jump to 100 on 09-30 was typed into the Test Shard's player editor, so his 28 of 29 successful chops afterwards say nothing about training. Hackworth is the puzzle. He climbed from 60.1 to 69.1 on 10-01 and then gained nothing for two more days, through about 7,800 more logs. No document explains it. A total-skill cap or a skill lock are the obvious suspects [inference].

Exploring spots

Choosing where to chop was left to the Thompson-sampling planner, which shipped on 2026-10-02 at 23:26 (). Its snapshot at 2026-10-06 12:00 shows how early in learning it still was. Of 132 candidate spots, 37 had a trip (38 counting Shelter Island, which the planner no longer lists). The 95 unvisited spots held about 87% of the probability of being best, the visited ones about 12%, because unvisited spots inherit an empirical-Bayes prior (3,166 logs per field hour) that sits above most measured posteriors (). shows the behaviour that followed.

Before the planner, the first 18 trips went to four seeded venues (Shelter Island 10, Horseshoe Bay 5, Corpse Creek 2, Terran 1). Hackworth's 10-03 trips still mostly revisited known spots; the bend toward the diagonal came with Outland Dan, who went to a new spot on 31 of his first 42 trips. That is the design working as intended ("Variation is required, not an inefficiency to remove", docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md l.411). The curve flattens at trip 112. During the evening shift the explore picks gave 262–392 boards each from small groves, and with the owner's goal set on boards the overseer switched to the planner's greedy pick (the best posterior net rate) from its 14th run on, passing it with --spot; the morning shift did the same docs/NOTES.md, Seer6 shift summary (ec339a1); 89130d8 added the greedy run to lumber plan. None of the 11 trips from 112 on went to a new spot, so these spot choices were not Thompson draws. Most spot estimates still rest on one trip: 25 of the 38 visited spots have exactly one, and per-trip logs are overdispersed by φ = 17.0 against a Poisson count.

Hazards and escapes

The harness lost a character to death twelve times, plus once on purpose. One small theft was seen (10 mandrake root). collects the safety outcomes. The incident swimlane and the Bastet timing are in , the CAPTCHA solver and its latencies in .

Two lessons sit behind the table. The recall escape works against creatures and against PKs seen early, but not against a mage who recalls onto you: Bastet's spell landed 1.9 s after he appeared, faster than a 2.0 s cast. That pushed the defence into spot choice and exposure time. And early returns, more than deaths, are what cost wood. At 2.98 sent-home events per field-hour, the expected time before a trip is cut short is about 20 minutes of chopping, and every early return pays the travel overhead again.

Money

Nothing was sold, so every wood figure in gold is a valuation. The 43 trip rows that carry a wood breakdown list 25,407 logs and boards (the newer rows break down the boards stored), 95.0% of them ordinary; at the board asking prices seen on 10-05 and 10-06 (ordinary 26.8 gp; goldenwood 70, verewood 170) they come to about 684k gp. Valuing the 5,860 trip-row logs without a breakdown as ordinary would add about 157k gp. These are asks, not sales docs/research/ECONOMY.md l.315; memory DB prices. Real gold moved in small amounts: 3,985 gp spent at vendors in 31 purchases (mostly a Mark scroll, a Recall scroll and a horse), and 2,785 gp earned by the secondary hunt loop over 36 New Player Dungeon visits and 173 kills, both unchanged since 10-04. Shackleworth's melee earned 16.92 gp/min, Hackworth's magery 6.06.

How far to trust these numbers

  • Small samples. 86 trips; 25 of 38 spots have one trip; all three PK deaths came from one player; the theft model is pure prior.
  • Accounting. Logs chopped ≠ boards stored. Hours nest: logged-in 87.6 ≫ agent-active 30.8 ≫ runner episodes 17.3.
  • Confounded progress. Per-day rates mix characters, skill, venue, pacing, Smart Harvest, the Harvest Aspect (lost with the 10-06 10:14 death) and, from 10-05 22:49, greedy rather than sampled spot picks; the planner rescales only for skill. The 10.4× rise (best completed trip against a three-trip run; ×7.7 day aggregate against day aggregate) describes where we ended up. It is not an A/B test.
  • One snapshot. Every number here comes from a read-only copy of the memory DB taken at 2026-10-06 12:00 CDT and the proxy captures cut at the same time, while a runner was still working; rows written after 12:00 are left out.
  • Unresolved: the shard label and the audit's 4,550-packet discrepancy.
