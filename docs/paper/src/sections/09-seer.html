The Seer: an LLM on the outer loop

The language model never plays the game. It sits one loop out: it wakes on a database row, reads the situation through a JSON command-line tool, picks the next deterministic runner and handles the exceptions.

From an API daemon to an agent that waits

The answer, committed at 23:47 49d433d: an omp session drives harness/ctl.py, the SQLite store is the bus, and the yield is ctl wait, a blocking call run as a background shell job. The daemon was "rejected for now" and could later replace omp on the same bus docs/PLAN.md:190-191.

The brief's first sentence stuck. Chopping, walking, fighting and storing are deterministic runners with their own guards, because "an LLM in the inner loop would be slow, costly and harder to keep stock-identical" docs/OVERSEER.md §1. The overseer reacts in a poll (~1 s) plus a model turn; anything that can't wait tens of seconds belongs to a runner. We learned it twice anyway. On 10-01 the overseer's improvised fight loop let Hackworth fall to 2 of 96 hit points among mongbats, and a programmatic hunt task replaced it that night f5008fd. On 10-03 a subagent fought by hand in Urukton Bluffs; the recall cast had knocked Shackleworth's staff out of his hands, and he died 15 s after one manual act attack, swinging his fists.

Decision

"Never fight by hand in a dungeon (user decision 2026-10-03): an LLM reacts far too slowly for combat … In dungeons only ctl run hunt fights; you scout, choose where it runs and watch." The same goes for lumbering: the Seer runs the planner's command verbatim, because "hand-picked arguments spoil the data" ().

The juncture bus and ctl wait

The bus is three tables in harness.db. junctures(id, t, source, kind, severity, summary, data, acked_t) is what runners want the overseer to know; chat holds the conversation (kinds message, thought, action, memory); meta holds tasks, cursors and a heartbeat. There are no queues, only cursors, one set per consumer, so each is restart-safe alone.

Runners post threat, pk_escape, captcha, death, speech_nearby (the job holds until acked), gm_suspected (a staff alarm repeats until acked) and rarer kinds; the task wrapper posts task_done/task_failed. ctl wait polls once a second and returns on an open juncture above its cursor with severity ≥ attention, on any task end, on a new user chat row, or when its timeout runs out, at most 20 events per wake harness/ctl.py:107-109. The "any task end" clause was the first bug of live use: on 09-30 an info-level task_done didn't wake the overseer, which "sat idle after its trip" until a fix landed about four minutes after the task ended, two minutes after the owner noticed b264824. The coding agent told the stranded overseer through the bus itself, with an ad-hoc notice kind.

task_wrap.py removes a finished task from meta.tasks before posting its juncture, so a woken overseer can start the next run at once. While a run is on, the prompt has the Seer wake every ~150 s (ctl wait --timeout 150) to read the status and the task log docs/OVERSEER.md:496-497, since 7a48fd5; only an idle Seer makes no tool calls and spends no tokens between junctures, with the shell job finishing as the interrupt.

ctl as the agent's API

Every overseer move is a ctl subcommand that prints one JSON object and exits 0 iff ok (). The guard rails live in the API, not the prompt: act accepts 29 whitelisted names and refuses raw packets, any act while a task runs, the captcha gump and button-less (honeypot-shaped) gumps. attack targets only hostile monsters, never below 30% hits; buy is capped at 50,000 gp a day. Free-text speech is allowed only during a speech_nearby hold, at most 120 characters, and must pass a regex that refuses words like bot, script, ai, proxy, claude or test harness/ctl.py:96-101. Every act mirrors a stock client gesture and is logged as a chat action.

Most of it grew on demand. The first live overseer, on 09-30, kept reporting "ctl has no …" and would not work around its rules: "I'm not allowed to edit the harness myself; I've asked the main agent to add an unequip/equip act to ctl. Holding still here until it lands." Each gap became a commit that day. Asked to farm gold by killing monsters, it declined under its no-attack rule; the owner overruled it within a minute, and the new policy was committed about five minutes later a0d78f7.

The prompt and the persona

docs/OVERSEER.md §5 is the operating prompt, and with 95 commits it is the subsystem's most-edited file (ctl.py has 73). On each wake the overseer thinks a reading of every event, follows a per-kind table (task end → next planned run; failure → retry once; possible staff → ctl alert first; captcha → don't touch), acks, and records what it learned. Since 10-03 shifts are continuous, ending only on the owner's word, a death, possible staff or a bug.

Memory in a shift

The Seer remembers through the store, not through its context. A shift opens with ctl know brief, which returns every pinned entry plus the standing procedures and whatever is near or in view; before a decision the prompt asks for one plain-language ctl know search; and what the Seer learns goes back with ctl know add, with a source and an importance. The operator's side: viz, Telegram, supervisor

The viz is a React page fed by viz_server.py, which polls the proxy's state port ~4 times a second and never touches its control port. Its rule is parity: "If the human can't see something, neither can the agent, and vice versa." A top-down map over the decoded terrain sits in the centre; beside it, the Overseer panel shows the owner's lines as royal-blue cloth and the Seer's messages, thoughts, actions and memory items as ink on parchment. On 10-05 the map showed Outland Dan "bouncing between trees for minutes after he had recalled home": a closed-gump leak had grown states to 1.8 MB, each SSE client had a 20,000-frame FIFO, and in a replay a reader limited to 1.5 MB/s fell 147 s behind after 200 s. One newest-state mailbox per connection brought states to ~170 KB and lag to ≤ 0.1 s 2536e09.

The Telegram bridge (10-02) is "one more party on the store bus". It forwards Seer messages and waking junctures, advancing its cursors only after each send, and turns phone text into user rows that wake ctl wait. Commands that act directly were rejected: "Everything goes through the overseer." A phone message ended the deliberate PK experiment: "Decline. You can stop for now, that's exactly the data we were hoping for."

stack.py (10-04) supervises the proxy, viz, Laya and the bridge, backs off 2 to 60 s, and adopts services it didn't start. On 10-05 it restarted a crashed viz twice (junctures #276, #300).

Laya: a local model for speech triage

Every line a player says near a harvest job holds the job until the Seer has read it (). The hold is safe but slow, and a gatherer who ignores a GM for about two minutes risks jail. So on 10-01 the owner asked whether a small local model could tell a staff member's attendance check from player chatter at once. Laya is a ModernBERT-large encoder (421M parameters) that answers typed yes/no questions over text in one forward pass, with no text generation. The harness asks it two questions about each line, with the nearby players and the recent speech in the prompt: check, is the speaker checking whether we are there, awake or human, asking what we are doing, or telling us to say or do something; and direct, is the line aimed at us. It runs in its own Python environment on the GPU beside the client, where a forward pass takes 59 ms against 901 ms on the CPU, in fp16 because Laya's default bf16 moved scores by up to 0.073, enough to cross the alarm threshold docs/NOTES.md "Laya speech triage".

Zero-shot, it could raise an alarm but not lift one. No staff speech had ever been captured, so the evaluation set (eval_triage.py) is 22 real player lines from the memory store and 25 GM-style lines we wrote. Across 16 question, checkpoint and prompt variants, no threshold could safely skip the hold: the one that kept holding every GM-style line would have skipped only 10 of the 22 player lines, and it sat exactly on the lowest GM-style score. The check question did separate the blunt checks. At 0.6 it caught 6 of 25 ("are you there?" 0.83, "This is a GM, please answer." 0.86) and alarmed on 1 of 22 player lines ("say 1"). The owner chose "shadow + escalate" from four options: every line still holds the job, and a check of 0.6 or more raises gm_suspected and the staff alarm the moment the line is scored. Each verdict is stored with how its hold ended, as labelled data for later docs/PLAN.md "Laya speech triage".

Synthetic data. On 10-04 the owner asked whether synthetic lines could fine-tune it. A search of the 97k captured Discord messages found players reporting that unanswered gatherers get jailed, but not one message quoting what staff actually say in a check. So every positive example would encode our own guesses: good enough to escalate sooner, never to skip a hold. harness/triage_data.py builds 1,057 prompts in exactly the format the live service sees, deterministically from a seed ():

  • GM checks aimed at us: 160 hand-written templates (presence pings, "are you a real person?", "type the word banana in chat please", "move one step to your left", jail warnings), each expanded four times with one of 24 names for us, one of five surface styles (as written, terse, lowercase, typo'd, chatspeak) and a scene: after silence, as a second ping, after our own line, or in the middle of other players' chatter. In 30% of them the speaker is hidden, not on screen. Our names never include the evaluation's TestWorth, so a gain there cannot come from name matching.
  • Look-alikes that are not checks: the same wording aimed at another named player, friends pinging each other with context that shows who is meant, players talking to us without checking on us (including back-and-forths with our reply), trade and guild-recruitment ads (also as cycles of 2–5 ads from one player), pet commands, "say 1" headcounts, and chatter in Turkish, French, Portuguese, Czech and Japanese.
  • Real player lines from the memory store, plus Discord messages as a capped 25% of the player side for register and typos. Discord questions in the second person are skipped, since in a scene they could be aimed at us.

Near-duplicates within a class are dropped. The labels follow the two questions: a check aimed at us is check 1, direct 1; a player talking to us is 0, 1; everything else is 0, 0.

Evaluation first. Before any training, the stock checkpoint (pinned to one hub commit) was scored and the result frozen. Besides the 47 evaluation lines, 104 real player lines were held out: everything an evaluation speaker ever said, the one real conversation aimed at us, and 30% of the other texts by a stable hash. None of them enters training, not even as context, and any synthetic or Discord line too similar to one of them (difflib ratio 0.85) is dropped. The deployment rule was fixed in advance too: false alarms on the real held-out player lines must not increase, and catches on the GM-style lines must improve. Training used Laya's own fine-tuning recipe on the top 6 of 28 encoder layers plus the decision head (99.8M of 421M parameters): 4 epochs in 288 s on the laptop's GPU while the game client ran, under a 3.2 GB memory cap ().

Run 4 went live as v2 on 10-04. Gentle openers now alarm ("Hello?" 0.49 → 0.93, "you macroing?" 0.20 → 0.70), the six blunt checks stay caught, and "say 1" no longer alarms (0.69 → 0.53). Some things got worse: a Turkish dungeon-farm ad now crosses 0.6, indirect checks fell further ("anyone home" 0.45 → 0.13, "please respond" 0.32 → 0.22), and the shadow-only direct score now fires on lines aimed at nobody ("bank" 0.04 → 0.82). The weakness was written down at the time: every positive, in training and in the evaluation, was written by us, so the gain shows the model learned our idea of a check, not Outlands staff. The one test that rests on real data, held-out player lines not raising alarms, moved by a single line (5 → 4 of 104), on a set that had guided runs 2–4. Live, the stock model raised two staff alarms on 10-02, both at guild recruiters; none has fired since.

Lesson

Each model here earns its keep where something deterministic checks it: the Seer's moves by ctl's guards and the runners, the Discord extractor by substring grounding and author recounts, Laya by a hold that fires on every line anyway. Where nothing checked it, a hand-fought battle or a reply to a stranger the owner judged suspicious, the model was the weak link.
