The lumberjack loop

Chopping wood was the first job the agent learned end to end. One hand-played demonstration was mined into facts, and those facts became a deterministic runner. The runner leaves home, chops at script pace, answers the shard's CAPTCHAs, holds for anyone who speaks to it, comes back through a house steward and stores boards. Every live failure became an offline scenario test.

Goal and hard constraints

The brief in docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md is short. The agent should learn the loop, run it and improve it: chop trees, recall home, enter the rental room, convert the logs to boards there and store them. The plan, committed on 2026-09-29 at 18:05 8b4291c, fixed a division of labour that held all week. We learn by demonstration and run a deterministic routine. An LLM is called only to compose, repair or reflect. The optimizer works only inside constraints that are not optimisation targets:

  • CAPTCHAs go to the human by default, with an auto-solve toggle since 10-01 ().
  • "Pacing is a floor, not a knob": no tightening of jitter, walk pacing, breaks or the daily cap.
  • No speech except "guards" after a guard flight. No packet a stock client wouldn't send.
  • On Shelter Island, never renounce Young status, so no travel there at all.

Three characters ran the loop: TestWorth (Test Shard: Shelter 09-29/30, Horseshoe Bay 09-30 after the owner renounced Young), Hackworth (a fresh Young character: Shelter 10-01/02, overworld from the afternoon of 10-02 to 10-03, plus a short 10-04 session) and Outland Dan (guild DTF, from 10-04). The runner, harness/loop_lumber.py, is now 3,443 lines. The planner that picks its spot and trip size is described in .

The runner as a state machine

Since 2026-10-04 e5345b9 a trip starts in the rental room, or within NEAR_LANDING = 60 tiles of the home landing (). The runner restocks, mounts, checks for a live trapped pouch and the Harvest Aspect armor, goes out, chops stand after stand, then comes home to convert, store and restock. The pre-emptions make it a machine rather than a script. A CAPTCHA hands off and resumes the same attempt. A creature triggers a walk-away of ESCAPE_RUN = 20 tiles, at most three per trip. A player at 2 tiles triggers a keep-away to 4. Three things end the trip at once with a recall home: a red (murderer) player in view, the trapped pouch popping (a thief at work), or low hit points. These escapes, shaded red in the figure, stop at the guild-house landing instead of going on into the rental room, because the room can refuse entry for 2 minutes after PvP docs/LUMBER_LOOP.md:675; the logs stay in the trapped pouch until the overseer takes them in. Each trip writes its episode row in a finally block.

Skills, not prompts

Under the states sit deterministic controllers: goto, smart_harvest(spot), convert_logs, enter_room, store_in_container, exit_room, plus deed skills that are designed but unbuilt. They generalise errand_bank.py, the Phase 3 acceptance task that walked to a banker and back. Every skill has preconditions, a success check against world-model evidence, a timeout and a typed failure. They share agent_link.py. Link talks to the proxy's ports and respects the agent gate. Mover plans 3-D routes (), charging 4× for a tile holding a mobile.

Decision

The LLM picks skills; it does not run the loop. It is called at start, on a typed failure the routine can't handle, and afterwards for reflection, so a steady-state cycle costs 0 API calls. We rejected an LLM per cycle ("nothing to decide in the steady state") and deferred Voyager-style LLM-written skills docs/PLAN.md.

The CAPTCHA solver

Harvesting raises a CAPTCHA every 5–10 minutes: a dialog with a three-digit number and a box to type it into. Three wrong answers block harvesting for 6 hours, and the shard can also read response time and long-run accuracy as detection signals ANTICHEAT.md §3, §8.8. So a wrong answer costs far more than a slow one, and the design follows from that. A human answers by default (the runner pauses and beeps), the solver sits behind a toggle in the viz header, and the solver is allowed to refuse. This subsection describes the solver for readers who want to build one: what the input looks like, the algorithm, how it was labelled and evaluated, and where it can still fail.

Finding the real CAPTCHA among decoys. Every lumberjacking attempt also opens a decoy dialog that carries the CAPTCHA's words ("Captcha", "Type the Value") under a fresh random gump id, with no reply button and flags that keep it off the screen. The human never sees one and the stock client cannot answer one, so any reply to a decoy can only come from automation inferred: a honeypot for bots that match on text ANTICHEAT.md §8.13. The store holds 5,087 of them at the snapshot, none with a reply button, and the harness's audits found no reply to any. The real CAPTCHA is gump id 1, with text entry 2 (at most 3 characters), a Guide button 1 and one more reply button, the submit, whose id changes with every CAPTCHA (594, 843, 528, 606, …). The first detector keyed on 594 and missed the second CAPTCHA it ever saw. Detection now requires gump id 1, text entry 2 and exactly one reply button other than Guide, and never looks at text harness/captcha.py:78-85.

The input is a point set, not an image. The digits are not pictures. Each digit is drawn as 9–18 tilepic elements, small item graphics placed at explicit layout coordinates, so the gump packet itself lists every dot position. The graphic changes from CAPTCHA to CAPTCHA (35 different ones across the 53 real CAPTCHAs) and carries no information. The digit is the arrangement of the dots, which are displaced from a fixed shape and sometimes include strays (, left). Reading a digit is therefore classification of a small 2-D point cloud, and there is nothing to OCR. The plan had a screen-crop classifier or a small CNN in reserve in case the glyphs ever came as pixels ANTICHEAT.md §8.8. With 7 labelled CAPTCHAs (21 digits) when the solver was built, a nearest-neighbour matcher over point sets fit the data better: it needs no training, its decisions can be inspected one reference at a time, it can say "not sure", and each new labelled CAPTCHA adds three references with no retraining.

The algorithm harness/captcha.py:

  1. Segment. Collect the tilepic coordinates (at least 18), sort them by x and cut at the two largest gaps in x, which gives three clusters. Each cluster must pass sanity gates (6–25 dots, a height of 40–120 px, a width of at most 120 px; the real ones have 10–17 dots, 63–82 px and 35–80 px). Anything else is not treated as a CAPTCHA.
  2. Normalize. Centre each cluster on its centroid and divide by its height, so position and size drop out but width and slant stay: \tilde p = (p - \bar p)/h.
  3. Compare. Measure the distance from the cluster A to each reference B with a symmetric, trimmed Chamfer distance, minimized over small translations:
    D(A,B) = \min_{\delta\in\Delta}\ \tfrac12\Big[\,T_{0.85}\big\{\min_{b\in B}\lVert a-b-\delta\rVert\big\}_{a\in A} + T_{0.85}\big\{\min_{a\in A}\lVert a-b-\delta\rVert\big\}_{b\in B}\Big]
    T_{0.85} is the mean of the smallest 85% of the nearest-neighbour distances, and \Delta is a 9 × 9 grid of shifts within ±0.09. Chamfer distance needs no one-to-one matching of dots, which matters because the dots are displaced and their count varies. Trimming the worst 15% in each direction ignores strays, and the shift search absorbs the centroid error that strays and missing dots cause harness/captcha.py:97-115.
  4. Classify, or refuse. The cluster takes the digit of its nearest reference among the 79 in the font, at distance D_1. Let D_2 be the distance to the nearest reference of any other digit. The margin
    m = \frac{D_2 - D_1}{D_2}
    must be at least 0.09 for all three digits, or the solver returns nothing and the human gets the CAPTCHA harness/captcha.py:118-142. Comparing against the nearest other digit, rather than the second-nearest reference, matters: two references of the same digit agreeing is not ambiguity.

Each digit costs 79 references × 81 shifts in pure Python, about a third of a second on the desktop, so about a second per CAPTCHA, hidden inside the deliberate human-paced delay described below.

Labels from the server. Labelled data came for free, because the server says whether an answer was right. harness/captcha_mine.py replays session captures, pairs every real CAPTCHA gump with the 0xB1 answer to it and the server's "Captcha successful." within 10 s, and keeps only accepted answers. Their three clusters become new references. Before adding anything, it reports whether the font as it was would have solved each new CAPTCHA, and with what margin, so every addition first serves as an out-of-sample test harness/captcha_mine.py:1-21. The dataset grew from 7 CAPTCHAs on 09-30 to 26 on 10-03 (12 sessions); the font has 79 references, 78 real plus one synthetic 0, with 4 to 15 per digit.

Evaluation. lists every test, from weakest to strongest evidence. Grouping matters in the held-out test. Digits from one session tend to look alike, so the test holds out whole sessions, not single digits. A leave-one-digit-out test would have been optimistic.

What the gate does and does not do. The margin protects against ambiguity: a cluster that lies about as close to a 3 as to an 8 is refused. It does not protect against novelty. The 10-01 failure shows the difference: a 9 unlike any reference was simply nearer to an 8 than to anything else, by a comfortable margin. This is the open-set problem every nearest-neighbour classifier has, and the defences against it here are outside the classifier. First, the font grew from real data: the second real 9, which the owner called "real funky" (86 px wide, with strays on both sides), was read right out of sample at margins above 0.6, and with it 194 reads correctly. Second, a human answers by default. Third, a rejected answer is noticed: the server opens a fresh CAPTCHA, the solver reads that one, and after its third rejection it hands over (--captcha-max-strikes 2 counts the rejections it carries on after) harness/loop_lumber.py:1768-1808, 3464. Three rejections are also the shard's block threshold, so this last line of defence leaves no slack; it has never been tested, because no answer was ever rejected. The digit 0 is the open case. Its only reference is a synthetic oval ring, because none of the 159 digits seen so far was a 0. If the shard drew digits uniformly from 0–9, the chance of that would be 0.9159 ≈ 5 × 10−8, so it almost certainly draws from 1–9 inferred, and the synthetic 0 has never been tested.

A synthetic score that fell as the solver improved. The 0.09 gate was set on the jitter test: with the first 21 references it accepted 99.2% correctly and refused 12% harness/captcha.py:17-24. Each later batch of real references lowered that number, to 98.1% with 37, 97.2% with 73 and 96.98% with 79, while every real out-of-sample test improved. Real digits vary more than the synthetic jitter does, so a jittered reference lands closer to other digits' real shapes as the font fills up inferred. On 10-03 the test's bar was lowered from 97% to 96%, and the real-data tests became the ones that count ANTICHEAT.md §8.8 "Dataset 26".

Answering like a person. A reading is not sent at once. The runner waits a lognormal delay with a median of 11.5 s (σ 0.38, stretched 12% per hour of play), chosen from the owner's measured solves of 7.7–17.5 s. It then checks that the client has not answered meanwhile, so one gump never gets two answers, and sends the stock 0xB1 with every text entry filled as the client would. Finally it waits up to 12 s for "Captcha successful." or a fresh CAPTCHA, which counts as a strike harness/humanize.py:52; harness/loop_lumber.py:1768-1808. shows the whole path.

The outcome. 53 real CAPTCHAs, 53 answers, 53 “Captcha successful.”, 0 strikes. The solver answered 43 (3 on 10-01, 11 on 10-02, 29 from 10-03 on) with a median of 13.25 s from gump to answer; the owner answered 10 with a median of 10.21 s. By design the solver is a little slower than the person it imitates.

Lesson

Four things carried this solver, and none of them was model capacity. The input was read from the protocol instead of the screen, which turned OCR into point-set matching. The model was one that can refuse: a nearest-neighbour vote with a margin, whose every decision can be traced to a reference. The server labelled the data, and each new label was first used as an out-of-sample test. And the evaluation that counted was real and grouped by session, while the synthetic benchmark drifted the wrong way. The margin handles ambiguity, not novelty; the policy around the solver (a human by default, a strike budget) is what handles a digit it has never seen.

Testing from captures

test_loop_lumber.py (3,109 lines) puts the real proxy in front of a simulated server. The simulator speaks the demo's packet shapes and the room's captured menus, and a scripted "human" answers CAPTCHAs. It serves decoys on every attempt, one solver-readable and one unreadable real CAPTCHA, a door that swings shut and moongates whose renounce prompt must be closed. The main run's two trips assert the invariants. The agent goes out by the door and in by the steward. It replies only to the CAPTCHA, gate prompts, runebook and room menus. It sends no speech at all, and every log not stolen ends up stored.

The working rule was that every live incident becomes a scenario that fails against the old code. The suite now holds 22 end-to-end scenarios, each in its own process with private ports, up to 8 in parallel (about 2 min critical path, 10 min serial). It also holds 12 unit checks, some of which replay live captures through the world model with no simulator ().

Lesson

The demonstration bought exact ids; it could not show other floors, crowded hallways, caves under trees, monsters or thieves. Each of those gaps surfaced live, got fixed once and was pinned by a test.
